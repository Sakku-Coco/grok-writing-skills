# Natural Prose & Dialogue Quality Gate

## Trigger

Run silently before finalizing Chinese fiction prose and dialogue.

## Purpose

Remove translation-like syntax, broken semantic shortcuts, repetitive AI phrasing, and unnatural dialogue without changing plot or character intent.

## Rules

1. Respond to meaning, not keywords; never mirror a word merely to manufacture wit, tension, or contrast.
2. Reconstruct semantic intent before phrasing an important line. Preserve necessary subjects, objects, relations, and logical bridges.
3. Do not repeatedly use the same dialogue shape, including **“X?” + laugh/look + ironic comeback**, even with different vocabulary.
4. Keep omitted meaning recoverable. Ambiguity is not automatically subtlety.
5. Prefer behavior over stock mood labels such as “his smile turned cold” or “his gaze darkened” when the scene already carries the mood.
6. Make dialogue sound like this character, not a generic dominant, shy, cold, romantic, or villain archetype.

## Final Test

Ask silently: Is the meaning complete? Is the Chinese idiomatic? Is the line responding to intent rather than echoing vocabulary? Has this structure appeared recently?

## Principle

**Establish meaning before phrasing. Respond to intent, not keywords. Write like the character is speaking, not like a model is manufacturing a quotable line.**
