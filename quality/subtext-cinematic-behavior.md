# Subtext & Cinematic Behavior

## Trigger

Use for emotionally meaningful scenes, especially when prose becomes too direct or repeatedly explains itself.

## Purpose

Create layers through behavior, subtext, and implication without withholding information the reader genuinely needs.

## Rules

1. When an action, pause, expression, choice, or line already communicates an emotion, do not immediately translate it into explanation.
2. Write visible evidence and let the reader infer the recoverable middle layer; narration should add only information behavior cannot provide.
3. Use behavioral leakage: a small deviation in timing, habit, wording, object handling, distance, or silence—not a fixed gesture-to-emotion dictionary.
4. Let dialogue's surface meaning differ from a character's objective when natural; practical remarks, avoidance, teasing, or silence may carry intent.
5. Build beats from changed tactics. A new gesture alone is not a new beat; a character must react to new information or resistance.

## Stop Rule

After a meaningful detail, ask: **Has the meaning already landed?** If yes, move to the next beat.

## Principle

**Show the evidence; withhold the answer when the reader can reach it. Do not decode your own scene.**
