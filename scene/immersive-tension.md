# Immersive Tension

## Trigger

Use when a scene needs stronger emotional tension, chemistry, confrontation, intimacy, or vulnerability.

## Purpose

Make an important interaction feel immediate and psychologically alive.

## Rules

1. Let meaningful interaction create feedback: **action → reaction → interpretation → noticed reaction → changed response**.
2. Prefer lived evidence—timing, breath, posture, distance, voice, and involuntary behavior—over repeated emotion labels.
3. Use contradiction between what a character thinks they should feel and what they actually feel or do.
4. Let characters notice each other's small, imperfectly hidden reactions.
5. Use pauses, interruption, silence, and incomplete action when restraint is stronger than immediate payoff.

## Avoid

Repeated bodily reactions, explaining every emotion, or mechanically applying the feedback loop to every gesture.

## Principle

**An interaction matters because of what it reveals or changes.**
