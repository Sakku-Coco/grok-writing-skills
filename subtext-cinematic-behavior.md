# Subtext & Cinematic Behavior Skill

## Trigger
Use for emotionally meaningful fiction scenes.

## Purpose
Create layered scenes through behavior, subtext, and implication without explaining meaning the reader can already infer.

## Rules

1. **Show Once, Do Not Translate It Again**  
   When an action, pause, expression, choice, or line already communicates an emotion, do not immediately explain what it means.

   Avoid:
   **behavior → emotional explanation → summary of the same emotion**

   Prefer stopping after the strongest layer.

2. **Let the Reader Complete the Last Step**  
   Give enough observable evidence for the reader to infer the hidden emotion, but do not always state the conclusion.

   The narrator should not solve every emotional equation for the reader.

3. **Use Behavioral Leakage**  
   Hidden feelings may appear through small deviations from normal behavior:
   hesitation, changed timing, interrupted habits, unusual wording, object handling, distance, or silence.

   Focus on **what changed**, not generic expression labels.

4. **Dialogue May Carry a Different Surface Meaning**  
   Characters do not always say exactly what they want.

   Let questions, practical remarks, avoidance, teasing, or silence carry deeper intent when natural.

5. **Explain Only When It Adds New Information**  
   Internal narration is useful when it reveals something the behavior alone cannot show:
   contradiction, memory, mistaken interpretation, private reasoning, or a genuinely new realization.

   Never explain merely to confirm what the reader already understands.

## Stop Rule
After an emotionally meaningful detail, ask silently:

> **Has the meaning already landed?**

If yes, move to the next beat.

Do not add another sentence that only restates the same meaning.

## Principle
**Show the evidence; withhold the answer when the reader can reach it.**

**Do not decode your own scene.**
